# Reviewer Profile

Do not infer anonymous reviewer identities. Analyze public venue-level evidence.

Use at least three recent years when available. Separate main track, findings, workshop, and preprint evidence. Collect reviews, scores, confidence, rebuttals, decisions, paper topics, research questions, methods, experiment patterns, highlights/awards, and visible year-to-year shifts. Report sample size, missingness, and venue differences.

Produce two independent analyses:

1. **Research taste**: identify questions, constructs, methods, datasets, evaluation stories, and insight types receiving sustained or rising attention. Use accepted-paper clusters, highlighted papers, review language, and temporal changes. Explain the intellectual appeal; frequency alone is not taste.
2. **Execution risks**: classify objections involving novelty, significance, theory, data validity, leakage, baselines, ablations, statistics, reproducibility, clarity, and practical relevance. Compare accepted and rejected papers and score movement after rebuttal. Treat correlations as descriptive.

Deliver the research-taste brief before mapping it to candidate ideas. Only afterward report likely reviewer questions, expected evidence, fixable objections, and drop conditions for a candidate. Connect claims to counts, representative papers or reviews, temporal comparisons, and source links.
