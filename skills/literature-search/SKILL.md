# Literature Search

Start from the question, not a preferred method or the user's prior paper.

## Search routes

Write a dated plan with exact terms, synonyms, competing formulations, observable behaviors, neighboring fields, benchmarks, method families, venues, and years. Run and log these routes separately:

1. direct problem search;
2. concept and synonym search;
3. task and benchmark search;
4. method-family search;
5. anchor citation search;
6. author and lab trajectory search;
7. latest 3-6 months of arXiv and current venue programs.

Use at least three independent source classes: official venue/publisher pages, scholarly indexes, and citation/repository sources. Lists, surveys, repositories, social posts, and lab pages may reveal vocabulary or papers, but verify each paper independently.

## Anchors and coverage

Build 8-12 anchors before narrowing. Cover theory/construct, method, measurement/evaluation, task/benchmark, and application perspectives when available. At least three must be top-conference/top-journal or canonical works and three must be external to the user's own benchmark. Avoid one-lab, one-citation-family, or one-terminology-cluster dominance.

For promising authors or labs, inspect the topic trajectory: earlier framing, present method, follow-up work, and changed or abandoned assumptions. Record why the trajectory matters; reputation alone is not evidence.

For every record save title, authors, verified affiliations when available, year, venue/status, DOI/URL, source route, exact query, authority tier and reason, relation to the question, and inclusion/exclusion reason. Preserve near-misses.

Separate peer-reviewed authority from recency. Recent preprints reveal movement but cannot alone establish settled results. Search snippets and model memory are not evidence. Report coverage and saturation by route, blind spots, failed sources, and retrieval date.
